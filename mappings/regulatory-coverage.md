# Global Regulatory & Compliance Touchpoints Index

The skills in this repository encode quantitative infrastructure engineering practices, not legal, tax, or compliance advice. This index provides a cross-cutting pointer map of where trading system implementations intersect with regulatory mandates, exchange rules, and tax frameworks across global jurisdictions.

> **Disclaimer**: Nothing in this repository constitutes financial, legal, tax, or regulatory compliance advice. Regulatory requirements governing algorithmic trading vary by jurisdiction, entity structure, and market venue, and change over time. Consult qualified legal, tax, and compliance professionals in your jurisdiction before deploying live systems.

## 1. US Regulatory Regimes (SEC & FINRA)
| Regulation / Rule | Relevant Skills | Key Mandates & Infrastructure Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **SEC Rule 15c3-5 (17 CFR 240.15c3-5)** | `sec-rule-15c3-5-risk-controls-us`, `kill-switch-and-drawdown-circuit-breakers` | Market Access Rule. Binds the *broker-dealer* with market access, not its non-broker-dealer customers. Controls applied on an automated pre-order-entry basis, under the firm's **direct and exclusive control** (d) — which is what eliminates "unfiltered"/"naked" access: credit or capital thresholds in the aggregate **for each customer *and* the broker-dealer** (c)(1)(i); erroneous-order price and size parameters "on an order-by-order basis **or over a short period of time**, or that indicate duplicative orders" (c)(1)(ii); pre-order-entry regulatory requirements such as Reg SHO and Reg NMS (c)(2)(i); restricted securities (c)(2)(ii); access limited to pre-approved persons (c)(2)(iii); immediate post-trade reports to surveillance (c)(2)(iv); annual documented effectiveness review (e)(1) and CEO certification (e)(2). The rule prescribes **no numeric thresholds** and names **no kill switch or position limit** — those are good practice and RTS 6 Art. 12 obligations, not 15c3-5 text. |
| **SEC Reg NMS Rule 611** | `us-reg-nms-order-protection-rule-compliance`, `smart-order-routing-across-venues` | Order Protection Rule: prevent trade-throughs on Protected Quotes, Intermarket Sweep Orders (ISO) routing. |
| **SEC Rule 2a-5 (17 CFR 270.2a-5)** | `multi-source-price-reconciliation-tie-breaking`, `vendor-outage-fallback-data-source-hierarchy` | Fair value determination for registered investment companies: oversight of pricing service providers, including approving, monitoring and evaluating each provider and initiating price challenges as appropriate; a market quotation is not readily available if it is not reliable. Applies to registered funds and their valuation designees, not to a proprietary firm's feed handler. |
| **SEC Reg SHO (Rule 203/204)** | `us-reg-sho-short-sale-locate-requirements`, `short-selling-borrow-cost-and-availability-modeling` | Pre-short locate verification (`locate_id`), threshold security tracking, mandatory buy-in timelines on fails-to-deliver. |
| **FINRA Rule 4210 (day-trading margin)** | `pattern-day-trader-rule-compliance-us` | The pattern day trader provisions of Rule 4210(f)(8)(B) — four day trades in five business days, the 6% test, $25,000 minimum equity, 4x day-trading buying power — were **deleted effective 2026-06-04** by SR-FINRA-2025-017 (SEC Release 34-105226). Brokers may continue applying them as house policy through the 2027-10-20 phase-in. Rule 4210(d)(2) intraday margin (intraday margin deficit, 5th/15th business-day deadlines, 90-calendar-day freeze) is the live requirement. |
| **FINRA Rule 3110(a), (b)(1)** | `regulatory-change-monitoring-service-integration`, `annual-compliance-attestation-workflow` | Supervisory system and written supervisory procedures "reasonably designed to achieve compliance with applicable securities laws and regulations, and with applicable FINRA rules". Procedures must track *applicable* rules, which is the supervisory anchor for regulatory-change monitoring. No monitoring cadence, tooling, or alert window is specified by the rule. |
| **FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-09** | `research-environment-vs-production-environment-parity`, `paper-to-live-promotion-checklist`, `new-strategy-onboarding-checklist` | *Guidance, not a rule* (26 March 2015): effective supervision and control practices for algorithmic trading strategies. Under *Software Testing and System Validation* it lists "Conducting any significant testing in a development environment that is segregated from production", "Conducting testing to confirm that core code components operate as intended and do not produce unintended consequences", and "Establishing a quality assurance process such that testing is performed independently of code development". Under *Software/Code Development and Implementation*: "Implementing a development and change management process that tracks the development of new trading code or material changes to existing code". Like RTS 6 Art. 7, it asks for **segregation and change control** — it does not require a test environment to mirror production, and names no numerical tolerance. |
| **FINRA Rule 5210 / 5220** | `wash-trade-and-spoofing-self-detection` | Prohibition of self-trades and disruptive quoting practices: pre-trade self-cross prevention, intent audit logs. |
| **CFTC 17 CFR 1.35(b)(5)** | `multi-account-same-strategy-fan-out`, `cftc-commodity-pool-operator-registration` | Bunched orders and post-execution allocation by eligible account managers: allocations must be fair and equitable with no account consistently favored, made as soon as practicable and reported to the FCM before end of the execution day, using a methodology objective enough to permit independent verification, with records reconstructing placement through per-account allocation. |
| **SEC Advisers Act Sec. 206 / Rule 206(4)-7** | `multi-account-same-strategy-fan-out`, `best-execution-record-keeping-global` | Written compliance policies covering trading practices, including allocation of aggregated trades among clients (adopting release IA-2204). No allocation method is mandated by rule; preferential allocation (cherry-picking) is charged under the Sec. 206 antifraud provisions. |
| **IRS Tax Code (1099-B, Sec 475/1256)** | `wash-sale-rule-tracking-us`, `section-475-mark-to-market-election-accounting`, `section-1256-contract-tax-treatment-us-futures` | 30-day wash sale tracking across accounts, Mark-to-Market election accounting, 60/40 tax treatment for futures. |

## 2. European Union & United Kingdom (ESMA & FCA)
| Regulation / Directive | Relevant Skills | Key Mandates & Infrastructure Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **EU MiFID II Article 48 & RTS 6** | `mifid-ii-algo-trading-compliance-eu`, `hardware-timestamping-vs-software-timestamping-accuracy` | Systems resilience: mandatory kill switches, pre-trade controls, OTR limits, microsecond clock synchronization (RTS 25). |
| **EU RTS 6 Art. 9 (Reg. (EU) 2017/589)** | `regulatory-change-monitoring-service-integration`, `mifid-ii-algo-trading-compliance-eu` | Annual self-assessment and validation, covering algorithmic trading systems, governance and approval framework, business continuity, and "overall compliance with Article 17 of Directive 2014/65/EU"; validation report drawn up by the risk management function. Annual is the floor — the regulation does not mandate continuous regulatory-change monitoring. |
| **EU RTS 6 Art. 5(7) (Reg. (EU) 2017/589)** | `reproducible-ml-training-pipelines`, `model-versioning-and-rollback`, `backtest-audit-trail-for-regulatory-review` | Records of "any material change made to the software used for algorithmic trading", sufficient to determine when the change was made, who made it, who approved it, and the nature of the change. A retrained model is such a change only when its output influences order generation or routing. A reproducibility manifest supplies the *when* and the *nature* (commit id, dataset and hyperparameter digests, environment pins); *who made* and *who approved* are governance records the firm keeps elsewhere. |
| **EU RTS 6 Art. 7 (Reg. (EU) 2017/589)** | `research-environment-vs-production-environment-parity`, `environment-parity-dev-staging-production`, `mifid-ii-algo-trading-compliance-eu` | Testing environments. Art. 7(1): testing of compliance with the Art. 5(4)(a), (b) and (d) criteria must be "undertaken in an environment that is separated from its production environment and that is used specifically for the testing and development of algorithmic trading systems and trading algorithms". Its second subparagraph defines a production environment as one "where algorithmic trading systems effectively operate", comprising trader software and hardware, order routing, market data, dependent databases, risk control systems, data capture, analysis systems and post-trade processing. The requirement is **separation, not parity** — auditing numerical parity between the two is a firm's own control, not a regulatory mandate, and no tolerance is specified anywhere in RTS 6. Art. 7(3): the firm retains full responsibility even when using a venue's, DEA provider's or vendor's testing environment. |
| **EU RTS 6 Art. 16, 17 (Reg. (EU) 2017/589)** | `risk-metric-recalculation-frequency-tuning`, `mifid-ii-algo-trading-compliance-eu`, `value-at-risk-var-live-monitoring` | Art. 16(1): monitor algorithmic trading activity **in real time** for signs of disorderly trading during the hours orders are sent. Art. 16(5) sets the only numeric latency bound in this area: "Real-time alerts shall be generated within five seconds after the relevant event" — so a risk metric feeding a disorderly-trading alert cannot sit on a 30 s or 300 s recalculation tier. Art. 17(1)-(2): post-trade controls must be operated "continuously", including "the continuous assessment and monitoring of market and credit risk ... in terms of effective exposure". "Continuously" is not defined numerically; a documented, monitored cadence is the practical reading. Neither article prescribes a VaR, Greeks or stress-test recalculation interval. |
| **EU RTS 6 Art. 12, 15, 16, 17 (Reg. (EU) 2017/589)** | `risk-limit-breach-escalation-matrix`, `mifid-ii-algo-trading-compliance-eu` | Art. 17: where a post-trade control is triggered the firm shall undertake "appropriate action, which may include adjusting or shutting down the relevant trading algorithm or trading system or an orderly withdrawal from the market" — the basis for a graduated escalation ladder rather than a binary halt. Art. 16: real-time monitoring by both the trader in charge and an independent risk control function, with "real-time alerts ... generated within five seconds after the relevant event" — a budget for the whole path, not for the decision alone. Art. 15: breaching orders must be **blocked or cancelled pre-trade**, so an escalation matrix supplements a pre-trade gateway and never replaces one. Art. 12: the kill functionality that HALT/FLATTEN decisions depend on. **No tier multiplier, sustained-breach window or acknowledgement SLA is prescribed anywhere in RTS 6.** |
| **EU AIFMD Art. 15 (Dir. 2011/61/EU) & Comm. Del. Reg. (EU) 231/2013 Arts. 39, 42, 44** | `strategy-committee-governance-for-capital-allocation-decisions` | Fund-manager risk governance. AIFMD Art. 15(1): risk management must be "functionally and hierarchically separated" from the operating units, including portfolio management, with demonstrable safeguards against conflicts of interest. Del. Reg. Art. 42 sets when that separation counts as satisfied (risk staff not supervised by, not engaged in, and not remunerated against the operating units), ensured up to the governing body. Art. 39: a permanent risk management function with the authority and access to do its job, reporting actual or foreseeable breaches of the Art. 44 limits to senior management. Art. 44: quantitative and/or qualitative risk limits must exist per AIF, aligned with the risk profile disclosed to investors (AIFMD Art. 23(4)(c)). **The regime requires independence, documented limits and escalation — it prescribes no committee quorum, no voting rule, no CRO veto and no per-strategy AUM cap.** |
| **EU Market Abuse Reg (MAR)** | `eu-market-abuse-regulation-mar-surveillance`, `wash-trade-and-spoofing-self-detection` | Market abuse surveillance: automated detection of spoofing, layering, wash trading, momentum ignition, and insider trading. |
| **EU CRR Article 105(8) (Reg. (EU) No 575/2013)** | `multi-source-price-reconciliation-tie-breaking`, `reference-data-golden-source-designation` | Prudent valuation: independent price verification in addition to daily marking to market or to model, performed by a person or unit independent from those who benefit from the trading book, at least monthly or more frequently depending on the market; valuation adjustments where independent pricing sources are unavailable or more subjective. A periodic valuation control for CRR-scope firms, not a tick-level feed requirement. |
| **EU MiFIR RTS 23 (Comm. Del. Reg. (EU) 2017/585)** | `reference-data-golden-source-designation`, `reference-data-change-notification-pipeline` | Financial instrument reference data. Art. 3: the ISO 6166 ISIN must be obtained *before* trading commences, and LEIs must be ISO 17442 codes listed in the GLEIF database — regulator-designated authoritative sources for specific fields, not merely preferred vendors. Art. 6(2): arrangements must enable a firm to **identify incomplete or inaccurate reference data previously submitted**, notify the competent authority promptly and transmit corrections without undue delay — which requires retaining which source supplied each field and what was rejected. Art. 5: competent authorities assess content and accuracy at least quarterly. **Binds trading venues and systematic internalisers**, not every investment firm. |
| **UK FCA SYSC 25** | `uk-fca-algorithmic-trading-systems-controls`, `kill-switch-and-drawdown-circuit-breakers` | Systems and Controls (SYSC) requirements for algorithmic trading: kill switch architecture, stress testing, change management. |
| **UK Senior Managers Regime (SM&CR)** | `uk-senior-managers-regime-algo-accountability` | Senior management accountability: clear lines of responsibility for trading algorithms, key person governance, algorithmic sign-off. |

## 3. Asia-Pacific & Global Regulatory Regimes
| Regulator / Authority | Relevant Skills | Jurisdiction & Mandate |
|---|---|---|
| **India SEBI** | `india-sebi-algo-trading-tagging-requirements`, `order-to-trade-ratio-fee-penalty-avoidance` | Unique Algo ID order tagging, broker pre-approval of algorithms, Order-to-Trade Ratio (OTR) penalty avoidance on NSE/BSE. |
| **Singapore MAS / SGX** | `singapore-mas-notice-on-cyber-hygiene-for-trading-systems`, `mas-singapore-algo-trading-guidelines` | MAS Notice on Cyber Hygiene (FSM-N22 for capital markets firms, FSM-N06 for banks): administrative account security, risk-commensurate security patching, written security standards, network perimeter defence, malware protection, and MFA scoped to critical systems and internet-facing customer-information systems. SFA licensing and SGX Approved Trader registration (no MAS algorithm registration exists), Clearing Member pre-execution limits, Forced Order Range and Force Key, SGX-ST circuit breaker band. |
| **Australia ASIC** | `asic-market-integrity-rules-automated-trading`, `australian-securities-exchange-asx-api` | ASIC Market Integrity Rules (MIR): automated order processing (AOP) certification, pre-trade filters, order cancellation authority. |
| **Switzerland FINMA / FMIO Art. 31** | `swiss-finma-algorithmic-trading-expectations`, `algo-trading-disclosure-to-exchange-membership`, `order-to-trade-ratio-fee-penalty-avoidance` | **FMIA (FinfraG, SR 958.1) does not mention algorithmic trading.** The provision is **FMIO (FinfraV, SR 958.11) Art. 31**, issued under Art. 30 FMIA and addressed to the **trading venue**: para. 1, the venue must be able to identify algo-generated orders, the different algorithms and the initiating dealers; para. 2, the venue "shall require participants" to flag orders, "record all entered orders, **including order cancellations**", and hold controls ensuring systems (a) handle peak volumes, (b) are subject to "**appropriate** trading thresholds and upper limits", (d) prevent Arts. 142/143 FMIA violations, (e) are tested, with precautions to limit the order-to-trade ratio, "slow down the flow of orders if there is a risk of the capacity of the system being reached", and enforce minimum tick size. Participant-facing duties come from the venue rulebook: SIX Trading Rules cl. 11.1.4 (report algo trading **to the Exchange**, flag orders, **a separate identification for each algorithm**, indicate the initiating trader, retain orders and cancellations) and cl. 4.3.4(2) (delete DEA client orders on Exchange instruction); SIX Directive 3 cl. 5.1.3 lit. h and cl. 10 (a verbatim restatement of FMIO Art. 31(2)(a)–(e)); SIX Directive 7 cl. 8 (mandatory Sponsored Access pre-/at-trade controls and the Exchange-provided **kill switch** — the only one in the framework, scoped to client flow). FINMA Circ. 13/8 mn 62–63 adds systems against false or misleading signals and documentation of strategy key features understandable to third parties. **Swiss law prescribes no numeric threshold** — no message-rate cap, price collar, notional ceiling, OTR figure, purge latency or timestamp granularity; microsecond timestamps are MiFID II RTS 25, not FINMA. Reform pending: the EFD *FinfraG-Review* (30 Sep 2022) proposes defining algorithmic/high-frequency trading (undefined today); FMIA consultation ran 19 Jun – 11 Oct 2024. |
| **Canada IIROC** | `iiroc-electronic-trading-rules-compliance` | IIROC Dealer Member Rules: pre-trade risk controls, marketplace participant obligations, short sale marking. |
| **Hong Kong SFC** | `hong-kong-sfc-algorithmic-trading-guidelines`, `shanghai-shenzhen-connect-programs` | SFC Code of Conduct: algorithm design, testing, risk controls, Stock Connect Northbound trading rules. |
| **Japan FSA** | `japan-fsa-high-speed-trading-registration` | FSA High-Speed Trading (HST) registration: risk control measures, system capacity management, regulatory reporting. |

## 4. Institutional Derivatives & Custody Standards
| Framework / Standard | Relevant Skills | Operational Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **ISDA Master Agreement** | `total-return-swap-synthetic-exposure`, `variance-swap-and-volatility-derivative-pricing` | OTC derivative documentation, collateral management, netting agreements, credit support annex (CSA) tracking. |
| **BCBS 239 (Basel Committee, January 2013)** | `reference-data-golden-source-designation`, `data-lineage-tracking-for-audit-and-debugging`, `data-quality-monitoring-dashboard` | Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting. Para 36(d): a bank should "**strive towards** a single authoritative source for risk data per each type of risk" — an aspiration, not a hard mandate. Para 36(c) with fn. 17: reconciliation means comparing outcomes **and explaining the differences**. Para 37: a data dictionary is a precondition for consistent definitions. **Scope: G-SIBs (para 14), extended to D-SIBs only at national discretion (para 15), and limited to risk management data (para 16).** Not a requirement citable at a non-systemic firm. |
| **CME SPAN Margin System** | `options-margin-span-calculation-global`, `options-greeks-real-time-portfolio-aggregation` | Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk: portfolio-based margining, intra-day margin call calculation, Greeks aggregation. |
| **MAS / ISO Custody Security** | `hardware-security-module-hsm-for-signing-keys`, `multi-signature-approval-for-large-transfers` | Digital asset custody: HSM PKCS#11 key management, multi-signature approval thresholds, withdrawal velocity limits. |
| **NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 (New York)** | `phishing-resistant-authentication-for-custody-access` | Sec. 500.12: since 2025-11-01, MFA for any individual accessing any information system of a covered entity, subject to the Sec. 500.19(a) limited exemption and written CISO-approved compensating controls reviewed at least annually. Covered entities are those licensed or chartered under NY Banking, Insurance or Financial Services Law (Sec. 500.01(e)), which reaches NY limited purpose trust companies and BitLicensees. The rule is technology-neutral: it does **not** mandate phishing-resistant MFA. |
| **NIST SP 800-63B-4 (US federal guidance)** | `phishing-resistant-authentication-for-custody-access` | Authoritative definition of phishing resistance as verifier impersonation resistance; AAL3 requires a non-exportable private key and phishing resistance, with 12h overall / 15min inactivity reauthentication guidance. Binds US federal agencies; a benchmark, not an obligation, for private custodians. |
| **NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5 (US federal guidance)** | `recovery-plan-for-lost-or-compromised-keys` | Sec. 5.5.2: a compromise-recovery plan **shall** be documented and easily accessible, and should name the personnel to notify and to perform recovery, the re-keying method, and an inventory of all cryptographic keys so re-keying can be monitored across every affected key. Guidance for US federal systems; a benchmark, not an obligation, for private custodians. |
| **CCSS v9 (C4 certification scheme)** | `recovery-plan-for-lost-or-compromised-keys`, `shamir-secret-sharing-for-key-backup` | 1.03.2/1.03.3: key material backups must exist and, at Level II, be held in geographically separate location(s). 2.04.1/2.04.2: a Key Compromise Policy and key inventory must be documented, the inventory reviewed at least annually (L2), and the policy **tested at least annually** (L3). A voluntary certification scheme, not law, and it sets an annual test floor rather than any shorter cadence. |
