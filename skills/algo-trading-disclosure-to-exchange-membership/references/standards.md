# Standards for Algo Trading Disclosure

This reference separates regulatory obligations from implementation controls.
Confirm the current venue rulebook, broker specification, and legal
interpretation before production deployment.

## Regulatory Touchpoints

| Scope | Practical requirement | Engineering implication |
|---|---|---|
| EU MiFID II Article 48(10) | A regulated market must be able to identify, *by means of flagging from members or participants*, orders generated by algorithmic trading, the different algorithms used, and the relevant persons initiating them. Article 18(5) extends Articles 48-49 to MTFs and OTFs. | The venue holds the obligation but discharges it through member flagging, so the member must preserve an algorithmic-order flag or identifier through the venue adapter; do not assume one universal FIX field. |
| EU RTS 6 (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589) | Firms must maintain algorithmic-trading governance, testing, controls, records, and the ability to identify relevant algorithms and persons. | Keep a versioned approval record, deployment evidence, durable audit logs, and a fail-closed pre-trade gate. |
| EU transaction reporting (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/590, Articles 8-9) | Algorithm identification and trader/client identification fields apply to transaction reports and use defined code formats. | Keep transaction-reporting identifiers distinct from venue order tags and validate both at their respective boundaries. |
| India SEBI framework and exchange procedures | Exchange-issued algo identifiers and tagging requirements are implemented through current SEBI and exchange operating procedures. | Make registration status and venue scope explicit; do not copy EU assumptions into NSE, BSE, or another venue adapter. |

## Engineering Contract

| Control | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Classification | `is_algorithmic` is explicit and owned by an upstream classification process. |
| Manual orders | Require `trader_id`; reject a contradictory `algo_id`. |
| Algorithmic orders | Require an exchange-facing `algo_id` and an `APPROVED` registry status. |
| Scope | If a registry record lists venues or a version, require an exact match. |
| Child orders | Preserve the parent algorithm identifier and reject a supplied mismatch. |
| Unknown state | Unknown, malformed, pending, deprecated, or suspended records fail closed. |
| Auditability | Emit a stable reason code, order ID, algorithm ID when available, and registry status. |
| Registry consistency | Snapshot the validated registry at engine construction and expose it read-only; update it through a controlled deployment process, never by in-place mutation. |
| Registry validation | Reject at construction: a non-mapping registry, a blank or non-string algo ID, a blank status or registered version, a `venues` value given as a bare string, and two keys that collapse to the same identifier after whitespace normalization. A misconfigured registry must fail loudly at deployment, not silently at order time. |

## Venue and FIX Mapping

The compliance engine validates the logical `algo_id`; the venue adapter owns the
mapping to the broker or exchange field. Never hard-code a tag number such as
`25013` as a universal MiFID II requirement. Confirm the current venue
specification, test the serialized message, and retain the mapping version with
the deployment record.

## Registration State Model

Use explicit states such as `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `SUSPENDED`, `DEPRECATED`, and
`REJECTED`. Only `APPROVED` is executable. A status transition must be reviewed,
versioned, auditable, and coordinated with the strategy deployment manifest.

## Authoritative References

- Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II), Article 4(1)(39) (definition of algorithmic
  trading), Article 18(5), and Article 48(10) (order flagging) —
  https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mifid-ii
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589 (RTS 6), in particular Articles 5-9
  (testing methodology, conformance testing, deployment, annual self-assessment and
  validation), Article 12 (kill functionality), Articles 15-16 (pre-trade controls on
  order entry, real-time monitoring), and Article 28 with Annex II (content and format
  of order records). Article 27 concerns general clearing members' disclosure of
  information about the services they provide and is *not* the algorithm-record basis.
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/590, Articles 8-9.
- SEBI, *Broad Guidelines on Algorithmic Trading*, `CIR/MRD/DP/09/2012`, dated
  March 30, 2012 — the foundational member-facing algo framework.
- SEBI circular `SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/0000013`, dated
  February 4, 2025 (*Safer participation of retail investors in Algorithmic
  trading*). Its implementation was deferred by `.../2025/108` (July 29, 2025)
  and `.../2025/132` (September 30, 2025), which set a milestone glide path and
  made the framework applicable to all stock brokers from April 1, 2026. Confirm
  the current position before relying on any date here.
- For the India detail — exchange-issued algo IDs, tagging fields, and the NSE NNF
  layout — use the `india-sebi-algo-trading-tagging-requirements` skill rather than
  duplicating it here.
- Current NSE/BSE/other venue operating procedures for algo registration and
  order tagging.
