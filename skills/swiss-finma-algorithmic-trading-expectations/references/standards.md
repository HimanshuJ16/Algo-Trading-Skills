# Regulatory Coverage — swiss-finma-algorithmic-trading-expectations

Jurisdiction: **Switzerland**. Venue scope: **SIX Swiss Exchange**.

The single most important structural fact: **the Financial Market Infrastructure Act
(FMIA / FinfraG, SR 958.1) does not mention algorithmic trading.** The provision is
Art. 31 of the *ordinance* (FMIO / FinfraV, SR 958.11), issued under Art. 30 FMIA, and
its addressee is the **trading venue**. A participant's enforceable duties come from
the venue rulebook the ordinance obliges the venue to impose, plus FINMA's supervisory
expectations for the institution. Citing "FinfraG Art. 31" for a participant duty is
wrong on both the instrument and the addressee.

## The duty chain

| Layer | Instrument | Addressee | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act | FMIA Art. 30 "Guarantee of orderly trading" | Trading venue | "A trading venue which operates a technical platform must have a trading facility which guarantees orderly trading even in the event of intense trading activity." Silent on algorithms. |
| Ordinance | **FMIO Art. 31** "Algorithmic trading and high-frequency trading" *(Art. 30 FMIA)* | **Trading venue** | Para. 1: the venue must be able to identify algo-generated orders, the different algorithms, and the dealers who initiated them. Para. 2: the venue "**shall require participants**" to flag, record and control. |
| Venue rulebook | SIX Trading Rules, SIX Directives 3 and 7 | **Participant** | The operative, enforceable participant obligations. |
| Supervision | FINMA Circular 2013/8 "Market conduct rules" | Supervised institution | Systems and risk controls against false or misleading signals; documentation of strategy features. |

## Numeric thresholds: what Swiss law does and does not set

**It sets none.** There is no message-rate cap, no price-collar percentage, no notional
ceiling, no order-to-trade ratio, no order-purge latency and no timestamp granularity
anywhere in FMIA, FMIO, FinfraV-FINMA, the SIX Trading Rules or SIX Directives 3 and 7.
The operative words are "appropriate" (lit. b, lit. e) and "if there is a risk of the
capacity of the system being reached" (lit. e no. 2).

Two specific misattributions are worth naming because they recur:

- **Microsecond timestamps are not a FINMA requirement.** FinfraV-FINMA Art. 1 para. 2
  lit. b asks only for the time of order receipt. Microsecond granularity and the
  100 µs maximum divergence from UTC for high-frequency algorithmic trading come from
  **MiFID II RTS 25** (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574), which does not
  bind a Swiss participant by virtue of being Swiss. That SIX's SWXess platform
  publishes microsecond timestamps is a vendor capability, not a legal mandate.
- **A kill switch is not a general FMIO Art. 31 obligation.** The word appears in the
  SIX rulebook exactly once, in Directive 7 cl. 8 para. 4, as an Exchange-provided
  facility over a Sponsored User's flow. The related capability duty is SIX Trading
  Rules cl. 4.3.4 para. 2 for DEA client orders. Both are scoped to client access. The
  EU analogue that *does* require kill functionality generally is RTS 6 Art. 12.

Where a numeric limit is needed operationally, it is the firm's own calibrated value.
FMIO Art. 31 para. 2 lit. b requires it to be "appropriate", which is why this skill
treats a threshold with no recorded calibration basis as a finding: appropriateness
that is not documented cannot be demonstrated.

## Venue scope

FMIO Art. 31 para. 2 works by obliging the venue to impose duties on participants, so
the participant-facing text is venue-specific. This skill encodes **SIX Swiss Exchange**
only. BX Swiss is separately authorised and publishes its own participant rules; its
published *BX Participant Rules* (non-binding English translation) contain **no
algorithmic-trading clause**. SDX Trading AG publishes its own Trading Rules. Auditing a
BX or SDX connection against SIX cl. 11.1.4 would assert an obligation that venue has
not imposed, so `SwissFINMAComplianceEngine` refuses any venue other than
`SIX_SWISS_EXCHANGE` rather than defaulting.

## Current status (checked August 2026)

- FMIA entered into force 1 January 2016. FMIO Art. 31 has been in force since then.
  The English texts quoted above are the Federal Council's **non-official**
  translations (FMIA status 1 January 2019; FMIO status 1 January 2020) — Fedlex states
  that "English is not an official language of the Swiss Confederation ... [the
  translation] has no legal force". The German, French and Italian texts govern.
- The wording is confirmed current by SIX **Directive 3: Trading** dated 17 November
  2025, **in force 2 March 2026**, whose cl. 10 para. 1 reproduces FMIO Art. 31 para. 2
  lit. a–e near-verbatim (see the table note).
- SIX Trading Rules cited here are dated 21 April 2023, in force 1 August 2023. SIX
  Directive 7: Sponsored Access is dated 1 February 2024, in force 10 June 2024.
- FINMA Circular 2013/8 entered into force 1 October 2013; last amendment 4 November
  2020, in force 1 January 2021. Margin nos. 62–63 are unchanged from the original.
- The FinfraV-FINMA copy consulted is the version with status **1 September 2018**. Its
  Art. 1 journal-field list is quoted for the point that *no timestamp precision is
  prescribed*; confirm against the current Fedlex text before relying on the field list
  itself for a record-keeping build.
- **Pending reform.** The EFD's *Evaluation des Finanzmarktinfrastrukturgesetzes —
  FinfraG-Review* (30 September 2022) records at item 30 that the terms *algorithmic
  trading* and *high-frequency trading* should, if possible, be **defined** in FinfraG
  or FinfraV — they are undefined today — and that the flagging obligation should be
  tightened to a duty of "unique and permanent identification of the algorithms",
  taking the EU regime as the model (Art. 31 FinfraV). The Federal Council's
  consultation on the FMIA amendment ran **19 June to 11 October 2024**. As of this
  writing no revised algorithmic-trading provision has entered into force; verify the
  current status before relying on the present drafting.
