## Numeric thresholds: what RTS 6 does and does not set

RTS 6 Article 15 sets **no numeric limits**. It requires that the four control
categories exist and are applied to all financial instruments; the values are set by the
firm under Article 15(4) on the basis of its capital base, clearing arrangements, trading
strategy, risk tolerance and experience, and are evidenced through the Article 9
self-assessment. Any "5% collar" or "10 messages per second" figure in this skill or its
reference implementation is an illustrative placeholder, not a regulatory threshold.

Article 15 does carry two non-numeric obligations that are easy to miss:

- Price collars must **differentiate between different financial instruments**, and apply
  both order-by-order and over a specified period of time (Art. 15(1)(a)).
- Maximum messages limits cover messages for the **submission, modification or
  cancellation** of an order (Art. 15(1)(d)), not new orders alone.

The three numeric figures RTS 6 *does* state are Article 10's stress-test multiplier (twice
the highest message and trade volumes of the previous six months), Article 16(5)'s
five-second alert deadline, and Article 28's five-year retention for HFT order records.

## Regulatory & Operational Notes

Applies to investment firms engaged in algorithmic trading that execute on EU regulated
markets, Multilateral Trading Facilities (MTFs) and Organised Trading Facilities (OTFs).
RTS 6 binds the *investment firm*; a firm trading its own capital without a MiFID
authorisation, or one accessing an EU venue through a broker rather than as a member, has
a different obligation set. Confirm applicability with compliance/legal before relying on
this skill's framing.

The United Kingdom retains RTS 6 as assimilated law rather than as EU law after Brexit,
supervised by the FCA — see `uk-fca-algorithmic-trading-systems-controls` for that regime.
Article numbering in the assimilated UK text follows the EU original.
