# Standards for Post-Incident Forensics for Suspected Key Compromise

Primary sources (all consulted 2026-08-27):

- **NIST SP 800-86**, *Guide to Integrating Forensic Techniques into Incident Response* (Aug 2006): https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/86/final — presents forensics from an IT rather than a law-enforcement view, across the collection / examination / analysis / reporting phases. NIST states it is "not to be used as an all-inclusive step-by-step guide for executing a digital forensic investigation" and is not legal advice.
- **RFC 3227**, *Guidelines for Evidence Collection and Archiving* (BCP 55, Feb 2002): https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3227.html
  - §3.2: "Where feasible you should consider generating checksums and cryptographically signing the collected evidence, as this may make it easier to preserve a strong chain of evidence." Also directs recording "the extent of the system's clock drift" and documenting each step.
  - §4.1: chain-of-custody documentation must record where, when and by whom the evidence was discovered, collected, handled and examined, who held custody over what period and how it was stored, and when and how custody transferred.
- **NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5**, *Recommendation for Key Management: Part 1 – General* (May 2020): https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-57pt1r5.pdf
  - §5.5: "When a key is compromised, all use of the key to apply cryptographic protection to information … **shall** cease, and the compromised key **shall** be revoked."
  - §5.5.2: "The worst form of key compromise is one that is not detected." A compromise-recovery plan **shall** be documented; its contents include an inventory of all cryptographic keys, the method for re-keying, policies requiring key-revocation checking, and "the monitoring of the re-keying operations (to ensure that all required operations are performed for all affected keys)".
  - §8.3.5: a key "**should** be revoked as soon as feasible after the need for revocation has been determined", may be revoked "on an emergency basis if there is reason to believe that it may have been disclosed", and the revocation notice "should include a complete identification of the key (**excluding the key itself**), the date and time of revocation, and the reason for revocation".
- **FIPS 180-4**, *Secure Hash Standard (SHS)* (Aug 2015): https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/180-4/upd1/final — specifies the SHA-2 family including SHA-256, the digest used to seal the evidence manifest.
- **NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500**, Second Amendment text adopted 1 Nov 2023: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/regulations/final_adoptions_fs/rf_fs_2amend23NYCRR500_text_20231101_alt
  - §500.1(f): a *cybersecurity event* is "any act or attempt, successful **or unsuccessful**, to gain unauthorized access to, disrupt or misuse an information system".
  - §500.1(g): a *cybersecurity incident* is the narrower subset requiring notice to a supervisory body, having "a reasonable likelihood of materially harming any material part of the normal operation(s)", or involving ransomware.
  - §500.17(a)(1): notice to the superintendent "as promptly as possible but in no event later than 72 hours after determining that a cybersecurity incident has occurred", with a continuing obligation to update.
  - §500.6: audit trails designed to detect and respond to cybersecurity events, retained not fewer than **three** years; records reconstructing material financial transactions retained not fewer than **five** years.
  - *Covered entity* means a person operating under a licence, registration, charter, certificate, permit or similar authorization under NY Banking, Insurance or Financial Services Law — which includes NY-licensed virtual-currency businesses, and excludes most firms outside that perimeter.
- **SEC**, *Cybersecurity Risk Management, Strategy, Governance, and Incident Disclosure* (Release 33-11216, adopted 26 Jul 2023): https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-139 — Form 8-K Item 1.05 is generally due **four business days after the registrant determines an incident is material**; the materiality determination must be made "without unreasonable delay" after discovery. Applies to SEC registrants only.

| Metric | Standard | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence integrity | Checksums SHOULD be generated over the **collected evidence**. The engine digests a canonical JSON manifest of every field of every analysed record; hashing record counts or a summary string is not evidence integrity. | Recommended practice; engine requirement | RFC 3227 §3.2 |
| Acquisition digest | The manifest digest seals the *parsed* records. Digests of the original artifacts MUST be taken at acquisition and carried as `source_artifact_digests`; their absence is reported. | Engine requirement, tracking RFC guidance | RFC 3227 §3.2 |
| Hash algorithm | SHA-256 as specified in FIPS 180-4. | Standard | FIPS 180-4 |
| Chain of custody | Who collected the evidence and when MUST be recorded and carried into the report (`custodian`, `collected_at`). | Recommended practice; engine requirement | RFC 3227 §4.1 |
| Clock drift | The source system's clock offset from UTC MUST be recorded. The engine widens unauthorized-outflow attribution by that margin rather than narrowing it. | Recommended practice; engine requirement | RFC 3227 §3.2 |
| Cease key use | On compromise, all use of the key to apply cryptographic protection **shall** cease. | Mandatory within the Recommendation | NIST SP 800-57 Pt.1 Rev.5 §5.5 |
| Revocation timing | The key **shall** be revoked, "as soon as feasible after the need for revocation has been determined", and may be revoked on an emergency basis on reason to believe disclosure occurred. **No fixed numeric SLA is prescribed** — any second-count target is a local operational objective, not a standard. | Mandatory (act); timing is "as soon as feasible" | NIST SP 800-57 Pt.1 Rev.5 §5.5, §8.3.5 |
| Revocation notice content | Complete identification of the key **excluding the key itself**, the revocation date/time, and the reason. The engine rejects key-material-shaped identifiers so the key cannot enter the record. | Recommended practice; engine requirement | NIST SP 800-57 Pt.1 Rev.5 §8.3.5 |
| Exposure radius | Re-keying MUST be monitored so that all *affected* keys are covered, not only the key that leaked. `derived_key_ids` drive `revoke_key_ids` / `rekey_key_ids`. | Recommended practice; engine requirement | NIST SP 800-57 Pt.1 Rev.5 §5.5.2 |
| Undetected compromise | A clean result MUST be distinguishable from an unexamined one. Empty logs, or a log window not spanning the suspected leak time, yield `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` and still mandate containment. | Engine requirement, evidencing §5.5.2 | NIST SP 800-57 Pt.1 Rev.5 §5.5.2 |
| Attempt vs. incident | An unsuccessful attempt is a cybersecurity *event*; only the narrower *incident* triggers the 72-hour notice. The engine counts rejected attempts separately from successful accesses and never merges them. | Definitional (NY covered entities); engine requirement generally | 23 NYCRR 500.1(f), 500.1(g) |
| Notification timing | 72 hours after determining a cybersecurity incident occurred (NY covered entities); four business days after a materiality determination on Form 8-K Item 1.05 (SEC registrants). **The engine computes neither** — it emits evidence and a determination time for compliance to act on. | Mandatory where applicable; not implemented | 23 NYCRR 500.17(a)(1); SEC Rel. 33-11216 |
| Evidence retention | Cybersecurity-event audit trails not fewer than three years; material financial transaction records not fewer than five years (NY covered entities). Other regimes differ. | Mandatory where applicable; not implemented | 23 NYCRR 500.6 |
| Forensic scope | NIST SP 800-86 is IT-oriented guidance, explicitly not a step-by-step investigative procedure and not legal advice. Findings here are engineering evidence, not a legal conclusion. | Scope limit | NIST SP 800-86 |
| Address normalization | Hex (`0x`) addresses are compared case-insensitively; Base58 (BTC, TRON) and Bech32 encodings are case-sensitive and are compared byte-exactly. | Protocol requirement | EIP-55 (EVM checksum encoding) |
| Amount representation | Exfiltrated amounts MUST be exact and keyed by asset symbol. Binary floats cannot represent decimal asset amounts, and amounts of different assets are not summable. | Engine requirement | Engine requirement (IEEE-754 representation limits) |
| Privileged-action defaults | `EXPORT_KEY`, `WRAP_KEY`, `UNWRAP_KEY`, `PUT_KEY_POLICY`, `SCHEDULE_KEY_DELETION` are **engineering defaults**, not a standard, and deliberately exclude `SIGN_TRANSACTION` (key use, not key exposure). Replace them with this signer's own action names. | Engineering default | Engine requirement |
| Recovery of funds | Reporting exfiltration destinations to exchanges and analytics providers is best-effort. Freezing is discretionary for those venues and is not a control. | Scope limit | Engine requirement |
