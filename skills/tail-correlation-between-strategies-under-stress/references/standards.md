# Methodology and Calibration Reference for Tail Correlation

## Status of the thresholds

**None of the thresholds below are regulatory or exchange requirements.** No regulator
mandates a tail-correlation limit for multi-strategy portfolios. They are internal policy
defaults chosen so that the detector separates genuine lower-tail dependence from
conditioning bias on simulated data; calibrate them against the empirical distribution of
your own strategies before wiring them to capital decisions.

## Definitions

| Symbol | Definition | Note |
|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | Lower-tail probability level. Default $0.10$; $0.05$ for extreme tails. | Must lie in $(0, 0.5)$. |
| $q_A(\alpha)$ | Empirical $\alpha$-quantile of strategy $A$'s returns. | Per-series, so differing volatilities are handled. |
| $\rho_{\text{exc}}(\alpha)$ | $\mathrm{corr}(R_A, R_B \mid R_A \le q_A \wedge R_B \le q_B)$ | Conditions on the **intersection** of the tails. Symmetric. |
| $\hat\chi(\alpha)$ | $\mathbb{P}(R_B \le q_B \mid R_A \le q_A)$ | Finite-level estimate. Conditions on $A$; ties can make it slightly asymmetric. |
| $\lambda_L$ | $\lim_{u \to 0^+} \mathbb{P}(F_B(R_B) \le u \mid F_A(R_A) \le u)$ | The copula coefficient. **Not** what $\hat\chi(0.10)$ measures. |

## Calibration baselines

These are the numbers that make a reading interpretable. All are reproducible from the
module's own simulation path.

- **Independence baseline for $\hat\chi(\alpha)$ is $\alpha$, not $0$.** An independent pair
  scores $\hat\chi(0.10) \approx 0.10$.
- **A Gaussian pair is not tail independent at finite $\alpha$.** At $\rho = 0.6$,
  $\hat\chi(0.10) \approx 0.39$ and $\rho_{\text{exc}}(0.10) \approx 0.22$, even though the
  bivariate normal has $\lambda_L = 0$ for any $|\rho| < 1$. A raw $\hat\chi \ge 0.50$ rule
  with no benchmark therefore flags ordinary correlation as tail risk.
- **$\rho_{\text{exc}}$ sits well below the unconditional $\rho$ under the Gaussian null.**
  At $\rho = 0.6$ the gap is roughly $-0.38$. Any rule of the form
  "$\rho_{\text{tail}} - \rho_{\text{uncond}} \ge k$, $k > 0$" is essentially unreachable.
- **Joint-tail sample size is the binding constraint.** An independent pair places about
  $\alpha^2 n$ observations in the joint tail: $\approx 5$ at $n = 500$, $\approx 25$ at
  $n = 2500$. At $n = 500$, $\alpha = 0.10$ and $\rho = 0$, over 99% of samples fail a
  10-observation minimum.

## Decision rule as implemented

Flag diversification breakdown when

$$\rho_{\text{exc}}(\alpha) \ge 0.70 \quad\text{or}\quad \big(\rho_{\text{exc}}(\alpha) - \rho^{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{exc}}(\alpha) \ge 0.20 \ \wedge\ p \le 0.05\big)$$

where $\rho^{\mathcal{N}}_{\text{exc}}$ is the mean of the same estimator over bivariate
normal samples drawn at the pair's own $\rho$, $n$ and $\alpha$, and $p$ is the one-sided
fraction of those null draws reaching the observed value. Detection is one-sided: negative
tail comovement is a diversification benefit.

## Sources

- Longin, F. & Solnik, B. (2001). "Extreme Correlation of International Equity Markets."
  *Journal of Finance* 56(2), 649–676. — Exceedance correlation conditioned on both series
  in the same tail; rejects multivariate normality in the negative tail but not the positive.
  <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00340>
- Ang, A. & Chen, J. (2002). "Asymmetric Correlations of Equity Portfolios."
  *Journal of Financial Economics* 63(3), 443–494. — Tests exceedance correlations against
  the values implied by a normal distribution, the benchmark approach used here.
  <https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=219495>
- Boyer, B. H., Gibson, M. S. & Loretan, M. (1997). "Pitfalls in Tests for Changes in
  Correlations." *Federal Reserve Board International Finance Discussion Paper No. 597.* —
  Shows correlation breakdowns are easily generated by stationary data with a constant
  correlation coefficient. <https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/1997/597/ifdp597.pdf>
- Forbes, K. & Rigobon, R. (2002). "No Contagion, Only Interdependence: Measuring Stock
  Market Comovements." *Journal of Finance* 57(5), 2223–2261. — Unadjusted conditional
  correlations are biased by the conditioning variable's volatility.
  <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00494>
- Coles, S., Heffernan, J. & Tawn, J. (1999). "Dependence Measures for Extreme Value
  Analyses." *Extremes* 2(4), 339–365. — Source of the $\chi(u)$ dependence function.
  <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1009963131610>
- Embrechts, P., McNeil, A. & Straumann, D. (2002). "Correlation and Dependence in Risk
  Management: Properties and Pitfalls." In *Risk Management: Value at Risk and Beyond*,
  Cambridge University Press, 176–223. — Gaussian copula tail independence.
  <https://people.math.ethz.ch/~embrecht/ftp/pitfalls.pdf>
