# Workflows for Risk Model Backtesting Against Realized Outcomes

## 1. Ingest the daily record

Record, for every trading session in the window: the date, the realised daily net trading
P&L (actual P&L / APL), the one-day forecast VaR as a positive magnitude, and the coverage
level it was produced at. Where available, also record the hypothetical P&L (HPL) — the
change in portfolio value had end-of-day positions been held unchanged — and the forecast
Expected Shortfall.

bcbs22 Sec. II urges banks to backtest on **both** hypothetical and actual outcomes: the
hypothetical series gives an uncontaminated test of the VaR calculation, while the actual
series can uncover risk measures that fail to capture real trading volatility.

## 2. Validate the window

Reject rather than degrade:

- dates that are not strict ISO 8601, are duplicated, or are not strictly increasing — the
  independence test reads the ordering, and a duplicated date double-counts a breach;
- a forecast VaR that is zero or negative — a broken feed, not a limit of zero;
- a per-observation coverage level that disagrees with the engine's — mixing 95% and 99%
  forecasts makes the count uninterpretable;
- a field of the wrong type.

Gaps in the date series (weekends, holidays) are expected and allowed.

## 3. Identify exceptions

An exception is a realised loss strictly beyond the forecast: `pnl < -var`. A loss exactly
equal to the forecast is covered.

Two rules modify the raw count:

- **MAR32.5(2)** — a day on which either the P&L or the VaR measure is unavailable or
  impossible to compute counts as an outlier. A NaN must not silently become a
  non-exception.
- **MAR32.5(1)** — when both APL and HPL are available for the whole window, count
  exceptions separately on each basis and take the **greater** of the two as the governing
  count. A partial hypothetical series is ignored, not mixed in.

Record the breach magnitude for each exception; bcbs22 Sec. III(e) notes that "exceptions
generated by trading outcomes far in excess of the risk measure are a matter of greater
concern than are outcomes only slightly larger". Missing-data outliers have no magnitude.

## 4. Test unconditional coverage — Kupiec POF

Compute the POF likelihood-ratio statistic in log space and convert under the chi-square
distribution with one degree of freedom via `erfc(sqrt(s/2))`. The test is two-sided: a
rejection with fewer exceptions than expected means the model is *too conservative* and
over-consumes capital, not that it understates risk.

## 5. Test independence — Christoffersen Markov

Build the 2x2 transition table over the T-1 adjacent pairs and compute the independence
statistic, then the conditional-coverage statistic as the sum of the two components, on
**two** degrees of freedom.

This is the step the count-based tests cannot perform. bcbs22 Sec. III(f) acknowledges the
phenomenon directly: when markets undergo a regime shift, a model that does not update its
volatility estimates fast enough can generate a run of exceptions that "would all be
occurring for the same reason". A degenerate transition table — no breaches, all breaches,
or a state that never appears on the preceding day — yields no evidence of dependence and
returns a statistic of zero.

## 6. Classify into the Basel supervisory zone

Derive the boundaries from the binomial distribution at the **actual** sample size: yellow
begins at the smallest count with cumulative probability at least 95%, red at the smallest
count with cumulative probability at least 99.99%. Never rescale the exception count to a
250-day equivalent.

Report the exact cumulative probability alongside the zone; on short windows it is more
trustworthy than the asymptotic chi-square p-values.

## 7. Attach the capital consequence

Only when the window sits on the published basis (250 observations, 99% coverage), report
the bcbs22 Table 2 scaling-factor increase, the MAR32.9 Table 1 total multiplier, and the
SEC Appendix E Table 1 total factor. Off that basis, withhold all three — BCBS publishes no
multiplier steps for other sample sizes.

## 8. Optional — Expected Shortfall diagnostic

With a complete ES series and no missing-data outliers, compute the Acerbi–Székely Z2
statistic. Report the signed value and its direction; do not attach a p-value.

## 9. Emit the audit report

The report must carry, at full precision: the observation count, expected and observed
exception counts and rate, the governing P&L basis and both underlying counts, the
missing-data outlier count, all four statistics with their p-values, the zone with its
boundaries and cumulative probability, the applicable multipliers, and the dated exception
list.

bcbs22 Sec. III(e) requires banks to "document all of the exceptions generated from their
ongoing backtesting program, including an explanation for the exception", and MAR32.12
carries this forward. Classify each exception by cause — model integrity, model precision,
market movement, or intraday trading — as bcbs22 Sec. III(e) sets out; that classification
is a human judgement this tool does not make.

## 10. Cadence

Compare daily; account formally each quarter over the most recent twelve months
(bcbs22 Sec. II; MAR32.3(3); 17 CFR 240.15c3-1e(d)(1)(iii)(B)).
